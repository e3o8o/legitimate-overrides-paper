# How peer review refined the Legitimate Overrides framework

Peer review for TERSE 2026 exposed structural weaknesses in the initial expected cost model. The reviewer feedback shifted the paper from an optimization argument to a calibrated decision-support framework.

## Refining the governance taxonomy

The initial manuscript calculated governance containment success by aggregating hybrid events, delegated pauses, and negotiated recoveries. This aggregation obscured the operational differences between authority types. The revision reclassified the Aave v2 incident as a Delegated Body action and the Alpha Homora V2 incident as a Signer Set intervention. The remaining 5 governance cases now explicitly represent hybrid or deliberative recovery. 

## Bounding the empirical claims

The first draft presented Prediction 2 as a fully measured collateral-loss model. The reviewers requested a tighter operational definition. The revision tests this using a scope breadth proxy. The new analysis shows that Account and Module interventions achieve 25.8% median containment success compared to 10.0% for broader Protocol and Network halts, executing 24 minutes faster at the median.

The revision applies explicit boundary conditions to the core variables. Centralization cost and blast rate function exclusively as structured thinking tools. Offchain resolution channels, including legal pressure and negotiation observed in the Euler incident, operate as external complements to the onchain matrix. Audit status is now explicitly designated as a coarse heuristic.

## Process as a driver of legitimacy

The original text reduced legitimacy to an ex ante centralization cost penalty. The reviewers highlighted that procedure also generates legitimacy. The updated draft adds a boundary condition confirming that legitimacy emerges actively through process and post-crisis conduct. The StakeWise renunciation vote and the Sui Foundation abstention illustrate how post-incident actions shape the evaluation of emergency authority.

The peer review process tightened the taxonomy coding and forced testable boundaries on the empirical claims. Thus, the resulting framework provides a concrete scaffold for protocol designers evaluating intervention tradeoffs.
